EASTERN TIME · EDT / EST

One region, two seasonal offsets.

EDT · GMT−04:00

In U.S. Eastern locations observing daylight saving time, EDT begins March 8, 2026 at 2:00 a.m. EST (clocks jump to 3:00 a.m. EDT), and ends November 1, 2026 at 2:00 a.m. EDT.

EST · GMT−05:00

EST resumes November 1, 2026 when 2:00 a.m. EDT becomes 1:00 a.m. EST. It continues until March 14, 2027 at 2:00 a.m. EST. In early 2026, EST lasts until March 8.

Under current U.S. rules, daylight saving starts on the second Sunday of March and ends on the first Sunday of November. Some regions do not observe these changes. Choose an IANA zone such as America/New_York to apply the correct rules for a date. Source: NIST daylight saving rules ↗

How do I compare timezones?

Open Time zone list, select a base on the map, and add one to five unique timezones. Choose a date and select an hour on the scale to compare the same instant across each row.

How is my information used?

Saved world-clock locations stay in your browser. Map tiles and library assets load from external providers; city search sends your search text to Open-Meteo. Using location detection requires your browser’s permission. No address lookup is performed.

What if the map does not load?

Use the localhost launcher and check your connection. External map services may be unavailable or blocked. City search and timezone dropdowns provide other ways to explore time.

How accurate are the clocks?

Clocks use your device time and browser timezone database. Keep your device clock and browser updated. Future daylight saving rules may change.
